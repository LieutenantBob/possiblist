## The 30-Second Pitch (for emails, intros, conversations)

> Most people are wrong about the state of the world — not a little wrong, but spectacularly, confidently, in-the-opposite-direction wrong. 74% of people think global poverty is increasing. It has nearly halved.
>
> Possiblist is a quiz-and-editorial platform that reveals the gap between what you believe and what is actually true. It's inspired by Hans Rosling's Factfulness, written in the voice of Douglas Adams and Bill Bryson, and it's becoming a movement.
>
> Members don't just learn the numbers — they see where the numbers come from, fund the organisations whose work appears in the charts (with a 20% match), and form evidence-based positions collectively. Not a subscription. A membership in a movement for the calm, rational majority.
>
> possiblist.io

## The 60-Second Pitch (for podcasts, press, detailed intros)

> Here is a fact: when twelve thousand people across fourteen countries were asked what had happened to global extreme poverty, seventy-four per cent got it wrong. Not approximately wrong. Precisely, confidently wrong — in the direction of the exact opposite of what had occurred. A chimpanzee throwing darts would have outperformed them.
>
> This is not because people are stupid. It's because the information environment is optimised for fear, not accuracy. Good news is structurally invisible.
>
> Possiblist changes that. It's a quiz that shows you how wrong you are about the world, then shows you what's actually true — with animated charts, verified data, and editorial writing in the voice of Douglas Adams meets Bill Bryson. Warm, funny, never preachy.
>
> But it's more than a quiz. Each article ends with "Where This Number Moves" — the specific organisation whose work produced the chart you just read, the smallest useful thing you can do today, and a 20% match on any donation you make through Possiblist.
>
> We ran our own research: the AI Worldview Benchmark. We tested four major AI models on the same questions humans get wrong. The finding was unexpected — AI isn't biased like humans. It's biased in a fundamentally different way, dependent entirely on how you phrase the question.
>
> The movement is designed to grow. At 1,000 members: a moderated forum. At 2,500: member-proposed micro-grants. At 5,000: collectively drafted, evidence-based position statements. At 10,000: an annual publication — The State of Being Wrong.
>
> The loud get organised. The calm, until now, have not. Possiblist is where that changes.
>
> possiblist.io
